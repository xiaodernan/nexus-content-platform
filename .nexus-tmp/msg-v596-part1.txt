v596 (part 1): 80 double-encoded punctuation sites are put back, the row that caught them goes green

verify:record-encoding was RED on master since f07c318: 26 chargeable lines, 80 sites, all in
docs/decisions/0016-*.md (amendments 545-548) and docs/evidence/m9/README.md. The sweep that
landed those amendments wrote them through the channel the row's writer model calls M1/M2: the
bytes were encoded UTF-8 and decoded as GBK, so an em dash became two characters and ate the
space behind it, and a section sign became one CJK character.

The repair is derived, not retyped, and every site names the channel that decided it:
- 15 section-sign and 4 middle-dot sites: one registered producer, and a 2-byte glyph consumes
  no follower, so the glyph alone goes back (byte delta -1 each)
- 49 em-dash and 11 arrow sites: seven and two candidate producers respectively, so the file's
  own spaced habit decides ( ' — ' attested thousands of times against a runner-up under 100 ),
  and because M1 CONSUMES the follower, the restore is glyph + space, not the glyph alone -
  the mistake the row's header calls the shape of 17 of its 24 ledger fixes
- 1 site whose follower survived as a wrong character ( red-cjk-green ) restored from the
  registry pair rather than the census
- 0 sites refused: no channel disagreed with itself

One pass is not enough and this is the reason worth keeping: the flank rule rescues a render
whose tight neighbour is CJK, so a dash sitting in front of a damaged section sign looked
sheltered and was NOT charged until the section sign behind it was repaired. The repair had to
iterate to a fixed point (79 sites, then 1, then 0).

Measured: verify:record-encoding green - chargeable lines 0 over 944 graded files, 191
flank-rejected look-alikes still seen, the ledger's 116 lines paired one-for-one, both planted
ends of the rule charging. Line counts and codepoint lengths are unchanged on all 27 lines;
no CR, file still valid UTF-8, no U+FFFD. The tracked repair machinery and ADR amendment 549
follow in the next commit; the ad-hoc scanner that produced this one is not being kept.
